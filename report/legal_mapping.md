# 2. Indian Information Technology Act, 2000

## Section 43

Section 43 addresses unauthorized acts involving computer
resources, including unauthorized access and copying or
extracting data.

Relevance:
If the conduct involves unauthorized access to a computer
resource or copying/extraction of computer data, Section 43
may be relevant.

---

## Section 66

Section 66 covers computer-related offences where an act
referred to in Section 43 is performed dishonestly or
fraudulently.

Relevance:
Computer-related fraudulent conduct associated with the
simulated digital evidence may potentially fall within
this provision depending on the facts and proof.

---

## Section 66C — Identity Theft

Section 66C addresses fraudulent or dishonest use of another
person's electronic signature, password or other unique
identification feature.

Relevance:
This provision may become relevant where stolen credentials
or identifying information are dishonestly used.

---

## Section 66D — Cheating by Personation Using Computer Resource

Section 66D addresses cheating by personation through a
communication device or computer resource.

Relevance:
This may apply where online fraud involves computer-based
personation or deceptive representation.

---

# 3. Indian Criminal Law

The assignment refers to the Indian Penal Code (IPC), 1860.
However, the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS) is the current
Indian criminal code.

For academic completeness, the historical IPC mapping and
current BNS position should be distinguished rather than
presenting IPC provisions as the current law.

## Historical IPC Mapping

Section 420 IPC:
Cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property.

Potential relevance:
Fraudulent transactions involving deception and dishonest
inducement.

Section 419 IPC:
Cheating by personation.

Potential relevance:
Fraud involving impersonation or false representation.

## Current BNS Mapping

Section 318 BNS:
Cheating.

Section 319 BNS:
Cheating by personation.

Potential relevance:
The simulated online-fraud scenario may involve cheating
or personation depending on the facts established by the
investigation.

---

# 4. Electronic Evidence

The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 contains provisions
specifically addressing electronic and digital records.

Relevant provisions include:

Section 61:
Electronic or digital record.

Section 62:
Special provisions relating to electronic records.

Section 63:
Admissibility of electronic records.

Relevance:
The investigation relies on electronic files, logs,
communications, metadata and other digital artefacts.
Proper preservation and documentation are therefore important.

---

# 5. Budapest Convention on Cybercrime

The Budapest Convention provides an international framework
for cybercrime and electronic evidence cooperation.

Relevant concepts include:

Article 7:
Computer-related forgery.

Article 8:
Computer-related fraud.

Article 16:
Expedited preservation of stored computer data.

Article 29:
Expedited preservation and partial disclosure of traffic data.

Article 35:
24/7 network for immediate assistance.

Relevance:
The simulated investigation is cross-border. Digital
evidence may be located in different jurisdictions, requiring
international cooperation and preservation mechanisms.

# 7. Important Legal Qualification

Legal provisions cannot be assigned solely from a crime label.
The exact offence depends on the facts established by the
investigation, the accused's conduct, intent, jurisdiction,
and applicable procedural requirements.

This project is an academic simulation and does not represent
a determination of criminal liability.

All evidence used in this project is synthetic.
